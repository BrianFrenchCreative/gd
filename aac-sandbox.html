When clinical care feels too complex, people don’t ask for help

American Addiction Centers had a critical opportunity to reconnect with individuals who had disengaged after initial outreach or discharge. My role was to develop compelling, demographic-specific visual hooks that drew vulnerable audiences in, paired with an approachable editorial framework that translated intimidating clinical care into digestible, empowering steps forward.

The Pitch

The Pitch

“We know you’re fighting the Devil...”

Driven by a belief that people in recovery are literally fighting for their lives, I developed bold, visceral visual hooks to match those stakes.

Art Direction: These conceptual ebook cover concepts visualized the daily battle through two distinct lenses: the male variation focused on the exhaustion of repeated bouts and relapse, while the female variation highlighted quiet strength and confidence.

Symbolism: In both, AAC’s clinical care was symbolized not just by a shield, but by a full suit of armor, representing the literal protection their tools surround patients with to hold back the ambiguous, stylized ‘devil’ of addiction.

Outcome: This conceptual direction proved our team understood the heavy emotional reality of recovery, winning the account and immediately opening a vital dialogue with AAC’s clinical leadership.

The Pivot

The Pivot

“Train to Win”

While stakeholders loved the emotional resonance, clinical directors cautioned that using ‘The Devil’ as the adversary introduced unintended religious implications. They flagged that framing the struggle against a demonic figure could inadvertently insinuate that addiction is a moral choice between good and evil, rather than a treatable medical disease.

That feedback directly shaped our pivot to the “Train to Win” boxing metaphor, preserving the same fighting spirit while honoring recovery as a medical reality supported by coaching, rounds, and resilience.

Developing the Core Asset

The core conversion asset required packaging complex therapies (like CBT and MAT) into an unintimidating format. While carrying the visual segmentation across demographic cover variations to maintain campaign continuity, I architected a modular editorial system for the interior pages. Using generous white space, clear typographic hierarchy, and scannable callout blocks, I organized dense clinical guidance into a reassuring, digestible roadmap to recovery.

    Trust requires an engineered foundation.

    To align stakeholders and guide AAC’s internal implementation, I built a comprehensive flowchart mapping out the Campaign Test Matrix. This document illustrated the complex logic routing for the personalized variations across every touchpoint—from the initial prospecting email and SMS drops to the dynamic landing page, ensuring the right segment received the right message.

    Following the campaign mapping, I translated the visual strategy into functional code. I designed and built the responsive HTML framework for the prospecting emails and the conversion-focused landing page. The clean UI execution ensured that regardless of how a user entered the funnel, the experience remained seamless, accessible, and optimized for lead capture.
